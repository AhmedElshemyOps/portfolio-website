1. The GM operates across systems

The General Manager receives fragmented evidence from Revenue, Guest Experience, Engineering, Housekeeping, Finance, People, Sales, Safety and Quality. AI is useful when it reduces fragmentation without erasing source ownership.

2. Fictional 60-unit UAE case

High occupancy, VIP arrivals, an out-of-order unit, repeated AC complaints, a late corporate payment, staffing absence and strong pickup can occur at the same time. The GM needs priorities and decisions—not disconnected reports.

3. Decision rights

The GM should not absorb every department decision. Clear delegation improves speed and accountability. AI outputs should name the decision owner and escalation threshold.

4. Evidence hierarchy

Separate source-system fact, department interpretation, AI hypothesis, management recommendation and approved decision.

5. Execution

Leadership action tracking should include owner, due date, dependency, status, evidence and escalation. Closed means verified—not merely marked complete.

6. 30-day GM operating system

Define daily/weekly/monthly cadence; standardize source ownership; build risk and priority reviews; strengthen decision memos and owner reporting; verify actions; remove low-value reporting.

7. Key takeaway

The best GM AI system does not make the hotel autonomous. It makes management attention more disciplined while preserving human accountability.

Prompt 01

GM Morning Brief

Global Prompt 231

When to use it

For controlled, evidence-based gm morning brief.

Required inputs

  • Approved source data
  • Controlled definitions
  • Named owner
  • Authority boundary

Expected outputs

  • Verified facts
  • Exceptions
  • Actions
  • Verification

GM Morning Brief

ROLE
Act as a hotel general-management and executive decision-support assistant. Support accountable leaders without replacing HR, Finance, Safety, department heads, the General Manager, owner authority or approved delegation of authority.

OPERATIONAL OBJECTIVE
Complete the task: GM Morning Brief. Use verified hotel evidence to support a transparent management decision.

TRUSTED INPUTS
Use only approved source-system reports, controlled KPI definitions, department records, approved SOPs/policies and documented management decisions.

ANALYSIS / METHOD
1. State period, source cut-off and decision objective.
2. Separate verified fact, interpretation, AI hypothesis, recommendation and approved decision.
3. Reconcile material KPIs before interpretation.
4. Keep quality, safety, guest and financial impact visible.
5. Identify exceptions, dependencies and conflicting signals.
6. Do not infer causality from correlation alone.
7. Do not infer protected, medical or sensitive personal characteristics.
8. Name the accountable owner for every action.
9. State required approval or escalation.
10. Define how closure will be verified.

OUTPUT
Executive summary; verified facts; exceptions; analysis; options; risks; recommendation for human decision; required authority; actions/owners/due dates; verification gaps.

LIMITS
Risk level: Medium, escalating to High for safety, employment, disciplinary, financial, legal, privacy or binding commercial decisions. Do not fabricate data, make disciplinary/termination decisions, expose unnecessary personal data or bypass delegation of authority.

HUMAN REVIEW
Relevant department head verifies facts. HR/Finance/Safety/Legal review domain implications. The GM or authorized leader makes the final decision.

STOP RULE
If source evidence, decision ownership or approval authority is unclear, stop and request verification.
Professional hotel trainer’s note

Keep evidence, interpretation, authority and verification separate.
