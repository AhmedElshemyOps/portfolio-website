Managing MICE Arrival Waves at Zayed International Airport

A delegate’s first scheduled activity may be the conference welcome dinner.

Their real experience begins much earlier.

It begins when the aircraft lands at Zayed International Airport.

It continues while the delegate moves through immigration, waits for baggage, searches for the event sign, meets the airport representative and boards the transfer vehicle.

For the guest, this may feel like one simple arrival.

For the MICE operations team, it can involve:

  • Live flight monitoring
  • Immigration and baggage uncertainty
  • Airport representatives
  • Meet-and-assist services
  • Delegate manifests
  • Hotel allocations
  • Coach planning
  • Luggage capacity
  • VIP arrangements
  • Accessibility support
  • Missing guests
  • Recovery vehicles
  • Client approvals
  • Hotel communication
  • Incident escalation

This is why airport arrivals should never be managed as a collection of flight numbers.

They should be managed as a series of controlled arrival waves.

An arrival wave connects a flight—or a group of compatible flights—to the airport team, the delegate manifest, the receiving hotel, the vehicle, the luggage plan and the next operational decision.

The purpose is not only to collect people from the airport.

The purpose is to give every delegate a calm, welcoming and dependable first experience of Abu Dhabi.

A successful airport transfer begins before the aircraft lands and ends only when the receiving hotel confirms that the guests have arrived.

#The operating scenario

This article follows the same fictional conference introduced in Article 1:

  • 500 international delegates
  • Seven principal arrival waves
  • Four hotels across Abu Dhabi
  • Twelve operating coaches
  • Two standby vehicles
  • Speakers and VIP guests
  • Several languages
  • Delegates requiring mobility assistance
  • A conference opening the following morning
  • A welcome dinner on the main arrival day

The airport operation covers flights arriving throughout the day.

Some flights arrive early.

Some are delayed.

Some delegates take longer at immigration.

Some bags do not arrive.

One passenger misses a connection.

Another passenger appears at the meeting point but is not on the final manifest.

These are not unusual events.

They become serious problems only when the operation has no clear response.

#An important airport note

Zayed International Airport’s official website currently states that flights operate through its main terminal, removing the need for normal inter-terminal passenger transfers. The airport also provides a live arrivals search by flight, airline or city.

For regular passenger pickups, the airport directs vehicles to the Short Stay Car Park opposite the terminal. Its arrivals guidance also notes that pre-arranged private-transfer drivers meet passengers at designated areas. A MICE organiser should not assume that public pickup instructions automatically apply to group coaches, branded welcome teams or high-volume event movements. Coach staging, permits, staff access, meeting points and loading arrangements should be confirmed directly with the appropriate airport, handling and transport partners for the specific event.

This article presents a professional DMC operating model.

It does not replace the airport’s official rules, security instructions or event-specific approvals.

Official references:

  • Zayed International Airport transfer guide
  • Zayed International Airport pickup and drop-off guidance

#1. Start with a clean sales-to-operations handover

The airport team cannot welcome guests correctly when the information received from sales is incomplete.

Before operations begins, the sales and project teams should confirm:

  • Total delegate number
  • Flight details
  • Arrival dates
  • Hotel allocations
  • Guest mobile numbers
  • Nationalities where operationally required
  • Language requirements
  • VIP and speaker status
  • Mobility requirements
  • Meet-and-assist services
  • Luggage expectations
  • Group equipment
  • Branding requirements
  • Client representatives travelling with the group
  • Emergency contacts
  • Additional-service approval authority

The manifest should not be treated as a static name list.

It is an operational document that changes over time.

The airport team needs to know which version is current and which information has been confirmed.

#Practical scenario

Sales sends the airport manifest seven days before arrival.

It shows:

  • 470 delegates
  • Six arrival waves
  • Three hotels
  • Four VIP guests

Two days before the event, the client sends an updated spreadsheet showing:

  • 500 delegates
  • Seven arrival waves
  • Four hotels
  • Nine VIP guests

However, the new file is sent only to the project manager.

The airport lead continues working from the old version.

On arrival day:

  • Thirty delegates are missing from the airport lists.
  • One flight has no assigned representative.
  • A fourth hotel is not included in the coach plan.
  • Five additional VIP guests have no special movement.

The information existed.

The operating system failed to distribute it.

#Primary response

The movement control manager should immediately:

  1. Stop using the old manifest.
  2. Issue the new document as the only approved version.
  3. Identify every affected flight wave.
  4. Reconcile vehicle and hotel allocations.
  5. Confirm airport staff positions.
  6. Check whether additional vehicles are required.
  7. Inform all hotel desks.
  8. Record any services requiring client approval.
  9. Ask each operational lead to acknowledge the new version.

#Backup plan

Every manifest should show:

  • Document title
  • Event name
  • Version number
  • Issue date
  • Issue time
  • Prepared by
  • Approved by
  • Number of delegates
  • Number of flights
  • Number of hotels
  • Summary of changes

A simple change summary can prevent major confusion:

Manifest Version 8 — Changes since Version 7

  • 30 delegates added
  • Flight XY208 added
  • Hotel Four added
  • Five delegates upgraded to VIP movement
  • Two mobility requests added
  • Arrival Wave 4 vehicle allocation changed

No airport team member should need to compare two large spreadsheets manually during a live operation.

#Sales lesson

The sales team should also define:

  • Final manifest deadline
  • Late-change procedure
  • Additional airport staffing rates
  • Additional vehicle rates
  • VIP service rates
  • Flight-delay charges
  • Extra waiting-time charges
  • Porter or baggage-support arrangements
  • Meet-and-assist inclusions
  • Emergency approval limits

Airport recovery has a commercial side.

Someone must know what can be authorised, who pays for it and how the decision will be documented.

#2. Build the airport team around zones and responsibilities

One person holding one sign cannot control a complex 500-delegate arrival programme.

The team should be divided into clear airport functions.

The exact staffing level depends on flight concentration, guest profile, airport arrangements, language requirements and the number of simultaneous movements.

A practical structure may include:

#Airport operations lead

Responsible for:

  • Overall airport operation
  • Coordination with the control room
  • Staff deployment
  • Major exceptions
  • Client communication
  • Final vehicle-release decisions

#Flight-wave representative

Responsible for:

  • One flight or compatible flight group
  • Delegate identification
  • Manifest reconciliation
  • Guest communication
  • Hotel allocation
  • Reporting missing or additional guests

#Sign or meeting-point representative

Responsible for:

  • Visible event identification
  • Directing guests to the meeting point
  • Preventing delegates from leaving with the wrong movement
  • Supporting guests who do not recognise the main event name

#Holding-area coordinator

Responsible for:

  • Organising ready delegates
  • Separating hotel groups
  • Monitoring waiting comfort
  • Preventing crowding near loading points
  • Preparing guests for vehicle release

#Transfer guide or onboard host

Responsible for:

  • Final boarding count
  • Guest welcome
  • Hotel confirmation
  • Onboard communication
  • Arrival handover to the hotel desk

#Vehicle dispatcher

Responsible for:

  • Vehicle and driver confirmation
  • Staging
  • Loading sequence
  • Luggage capacity
  • Release approval
  • Replacement vehicles

#Floater or recovery representative

Responsible for:

  • Delayed passengers
  • Baggage cases
  • Unexpected guests
  • Staff relief
  • Additional movements
  • Urgent reassignment

#Practical scenario

Three flights are expected within 40 minutes.

The airport team includes:

  • One airport lead
  • Three flight representatives
  • Two sign staff
  • One dispatcher
  • One floater

Flight One releases its passengers quickly.

Flight Two has a baggage delay.

Flight Three lands early.

The airport lead asks the floater to help Flight One load its coach.

A few minutes later, a passenger from Flight Two reports missing baggage, while Flight Three’s first delegates reach the meeting point.

The floater is now occupied at the wrong pressure point.

#Primary response

The airport lead should prioritise responsibilities based on risk:

  1. Flight One already has a complete team and is ready for controlled release.
  2. Flight Two requires one representative to remain with the baggage case.
  3. Flight Three requires visible meeting-point coverage immediately.
  4. The floater should be reassigned to Flight Three.
  5. The airport lead or holding coordinator can support Flight One’s final release.
  6. The control room should be informed that recovery capacity is temporarily reduced.

#Backup plan

Before the first arrival, every critical function should have:

  • A primary employee
  • A secondary employee
  • A supervisor
  • A break arrangement
  • A communication channel
  • A replacement procedure
  • Access to the current manifest

The airport lead should also know which functions cannot be left uncovered.

Visible meeting-point coverage, guest safety, special-assistance support and controlled vehicle release should normally take priority over non-urgent administrative work.

A flight number alone is not enough to control a group arrival.

Every flight wave should have one concise operating card.

#Practical scenario

Arrival Wave 3 includes 52 delegates from Frankfurt.

The coach has 53 passenger seats.

The planner assumes the vehicle is sufficient.

On arrival, the team discovers:

  • Most delegates have two large bags.
  • Six delegates are carrying exhibition materials.
  • One speaker has additional presentation equipment.
  • Several bags cannot fit safely in the luggage compartments.

The coach has enough seats.

It does not have enough practical movement capacity.

#Primary response

The dispatcher should:

  1. Stop loading before luggage blocks safe areas.
  2. Count standard luggage, oversized luggage and equipment separately.
  3. Keep delegates informed.
  4. Activate the planned luggage vehicle if available.
  5. Confirm that all luggage is labelled by hotel or movement.
  6. Record which vehicle carries the separate luggage.
  7. Inform the receiving hotel.
  8. Avoid separating essential medication or personal items from guests.

#Backup plan

The arrival-wave card should include:

  • Expected bags per person
  • Oversized equipment
  • Exhibition materials
  • Wheelchairs or mobility devices
  • Child equipment where relevant
  • Luggage-vehicle requirement
  • Hotel luggage-contact person

Passenger capacity and baggage capacity should be checked separately.

A 50-seat coach is not automatically suitable for 50 international delegates with conference luggage.

#4. Monitor the arrival before it becomes an exception

Airport operations should not begin when the aircraft appears on the arrivals screen.

Flight monitoring should start before the airport team is deployed.

A practical monitoring rhythm may include:

#Twenty-four hours before arrival

  • Confirm the flight is operating.
  • Review major schedule changes.
  • Confirm delegate numbers.
  • Check hotel allocations.
  • Confirm airport staff.
  • Confirm vehicles and drivers.
  • Confirm special assistance.
  • Review meeting and loading points.

#Six hours before arrival

  • Check live flight status.
  • Confirm operating aircraft or significant changes where available.
  • Review connecting-flight risks.
  • Confirm vehicle dispatch timing.
  • Confirm hotel readiness.

#Two hours before arrival

  • Check updated arrival time.
  • Position airport staff.
  • Confirm signs.
  • Confirm communication channels.
  • Confirm vehicle access and staging.
  • Review open exceptions.

#After landing

  • Record actual landing time.
  • Estimate immigration and baggage release.
  • Update the hotel and vehicle teams.
  • Monitor the first passenger’s arrival at the meeting point.
  • Begin the physical guest count.

Zayed International Airport provides a live arrivals search, but a professional operation should also use airline information, client updates and approved handling-partner information when available.

Official reference: Zayed International Airport live arrivals

#Practical scenario

A flight is shown as on time when the airport team leaves the operations office.

During the journey to the airport, the estimated arrival changes by 90 minutes.

The airport representatives arrive too early.

The coach driver is also dispatched early and begins waiting.

By the time guests are ready:

  • The airport staff are approaching overtime.
  • The driver may exceed the planned duty period.
  • Another movement needs the coach.
  • The waiting charge is increasing.

#Primary response

The airport lead should:

  1. Confirm the new live arrival time.
  2. Inform the movement control room.
  3. Review whether airport staff should remain or rotate.
  4. Check the driver’s legal and operational duty limits.
  5. Delay or reassign the vehicle where possible.
  6. Protect coverage for other flights.
  7. Record potential additional cost.
  8. Inform the receiving hotel.

#Backup plan

The operating plan should define:

  • When staff are deployed
  • When vehicles are dispatched
  • What delay triggers rescheduling
  • How overtime is approved
  • Which employee remains as minimum coverage
  • Which vehicle can be reassigned
  • When the hotel should be informed

The purpose of flight monitoring is not only to observe a delay.

It is to reduce the cost and guest impact before the flight lands.

#5. When a flight arrives early

Early arrivals are often treated as good news.

Operationally, they can create the same level of disruption as late flights.

An early arrival may mean:

  • Airport representatives are not yet in position.
  • The coach has not been dispatched.
  • The hotel is not ready.
  • Hospitality desks have not opened.
  • Rooms are unavailable.
  • VIP vehicles are still completing another movement.

#Practical scenario

A flight carrying 36 delegates is scheduled to arrive at 09:00.

It lands at 08:15.

The first guests reach the meeting point at 08:55.

The coach is scheduled to arrive at 09:25.

The receiving hotel has confirmed that rooms will not be available before midday.

#Primary response

The airport lead should first determine:

  • How many delegates are ready?
  • How long until the main coach arrives?
  • Is the waiting point comfortable?
  • Is the hotel ready to receive early guests?
  • Is a smaller vehicle available?
  • Are any speakers or special-needs delegates affected?
  • Will an early release improve the guest experience?

The team may decide to keep the group together when:

  • The expected wait is limited
  • Seating and facilities are available
  • Most delegates are still collecting baggage
  • The hotel cannot yet receive them
  • The guests receive clear updates

#Suggested guest message

Welcome to Abu Dhabi. Your flight arrived earlier than expected, and our transfer is being brought forward. Please remain with our team at the meeting point. Refreshments and facilities are available nearby, and we will update you again at 09:05.

#Backup plan

Possible recovery options include:

Bring the coach forward

Use when the driver and vehicle can be safely reassigned without harming another movement.

Release a priority group

Use a smaller vehicle for:

  • Speakers
  • VIPs
  • Older delegates
  • Guests with mobility needs
  • Delegates with an urgent meeting

Activate an airport hospitality plan

When the hotel is not ready, the airport or event plan may include:

  • Comfortable waiting
  • Refreshments
  • Meet-and-assist support
  • A revised orientation
  • A hotel hospitality room
  • Luggage storage
  • A short optional programme when practical

Adjust the hotel arrival

The hotel desk should be told:

  • New expected arrival time
  • Number of early guests
  • Priority guests
  • Luggage requirements
  • Room-readiness issue
  • Agreed guest message

An early flight should not simply transfer the waiting problem from the airport to the hotel lobby.

#6. When a flight is delayed

A delayed flight affects more than the passengers on that aircraft.

It may affect:

  • Shared coaches
  • Airport staffing
  • Driver duty hours
  • Hotel desks
  • Welcome meals
  • Registration
  • Speaker schedules
  • Evening functions
  • Other vehicle movements

#Practical scenario

Thirty-eight delegates from Paris are grouped with 25 delegates arriving from another European city.

The two flights were expected to release passengers within 30 minutes of each other.

The Paris flight is delayed by three hours.

The 25 delegates from the other flight are ready.

#Primary response

The airport lead should separate the two flight waves.

The ready passengers should normally not wait for three hours.

The team should:

  1. Release the 25 ready delegates on the planned coach.
  2. Keep one representative assigned to the Paris flight.
  3. Reassign a recovery vehicle.
  4. Update the hotel.
  5. Review the welcome-dinner schedule.
  6. Check driver hours and overtime.
  7. Confirm additional costs.
  8. Send the delayed group an updated message when possible.

#Suggested pre-arrival message

Welcome to Abu Dhabi. We are monitoring your revised arrival time. Our representative will meet you at the confirmed meeting point after immigration and baggage collection. Your hotel transfer remains arranged, and we will share the updated vehicle details after landing.

#Backup plan

Every delayed flight should trigger a review of:

  • Airport staff coverage
  • Vehicle allocation
  • Hotel arrival time
  • Meal availability
  • Registration opening
  • Speaker commitments
  • Guest communication
  • Additional costs
  • Overnight risk

#Decision rule example

A practical rule could be:

Flights with an expected passenger-release difference greater than the approved waiting limit will be separated into different movements.

The exact waiting limit should be agreed for the event.

It may vary according to:

  • Guest profile
  • Time of day
  • Meal programme
  • Hotel readiness
  • Vehicle availability
  • Client service promise

The team should not invent the rule while 25 tired passengers are standing at the airport.

#7. When several flights release at the same time

Scheduled landing times do not always predict actual passenger-release times.

One flight may arrive first but face slow immigration.

Another may land later but release passengers quickly.

Several groups can therefore reach the meeting area at the same time.

#Practical scenario

Three flights are planned as separate arrival waves:

  • Wave 2: 44 delegates
  • Wave 3: 51 delegates
  • Wave 4: 39 delegates

Due to different immigration and baggage times, more than 100 delegates reach the public meeting area within 15 minutes.

The challenges include:

  • Several event signs
  • Four hotel allocations
  • Limited holding space
  • Different vehicles
  • Guests following the wrong representative
  • Luggage blocking the flow
  • VIP guests mixed into the main crowd

#Primary response

The airport lead should activate zone control.

Zone 1: Identification

Sign staff identify event delegates and direct them to the correct wave.

Zone 2: Manifest confirmation

Representatives confirm guest identity and hotel allocation.

Zone 3: Holding

Delegates wait in organised hotel or movement groups.

Zone 4: Loading preparation

The dispatcher calls only the movement ready for loading.

The team should avoid bringing every coach and every delegate to the loading area simultaneously.

#Suggested guest message

Welcome to Abu Dhabi. We are currently receiving several conference flights. Please remain with your hotel group and follow the movement sign provided by our team. Your representative will call your coach when it is ready for loading.

#Backup plan

For high-concentration arrival periods, prepare:

  • Additional sign staff
  • A larger holding structure
  • Colour-and-number movement identities
  • Spare signs
  • Extra radios or communication devices
  • One floater per major pressure window
  • Staggered coach calls
  • Separate VIP handling
  • Hotel-labelled luggage tags
  • A predefined overflow holding area

The airport team should manage the crowd before the crowd reaches the coach door.

#8. When immigration takes longer than expected

Once the flight lands, the DMC cannot control the immigration process.

It can control how it responds to uncertainty.

The team should avoid assuming that every passenger will complete arrival procedures at the same speed.

Possible reasons for delay include:

  • Visa or document review
  • Long immigration queues
  • Passport concerns
  • First-time entry questions
  • Families travelling together
  • Individual security procedures
  • A guest moving more slowly
  • A guest using special assistance

#Practical scenario

A group of 46 delegates arrives.

Forty-two reach the meeting point.

Four are still inside the controlled arrival process.

The airport representative knows that two of the missing guests are travelling together, but there is no confirmed information about the other two.

The coach has waited for 25 minutes.

#Primary response

The representative should first check:

  • Were all four passengers confirmed onboard by the client or airline information?
  • Has anyone in the group contacted them?
  • Do they have working mobile numbers?
  • Are they travelling with colleagues?
  • Is a special-assistance request involved?
  • Is a second vehicle available?
  • What is the latest approved coach-release time?

The representative should not attempt to enter restricted areas or interfere with official procedures.

#Backup plan

At the agreed decision time:

  1. Keep one representative responsible for the four guests.
  2. Release the main coach when approved.
  3. Use a recovery vehicle for the remaining guests.
  4. Inform the hotel desk.
  5. Record the missing passengers as unresolved—not as no-shows.
  6. Continue approved contact attempts.
  7. Escalate when the delay exceeds the agreed threshold.

#Suggested message for the main group

A small number of delegates are still completing arrival procedures. A member of our team will remain at the airport to assist them, and their hotel transfer is arranged separately. We will now continue to the hotel.

#Important distinction

A guest who has not reached the meeting point is not automatically absent.

Possible statuses should include:

  • Still in immigration
  • Still at baggage claim
  • Baggage case
  • Contacted and approaching
  • Left the airport independently
  • Missed flight
  • No confirmed information
  • Confirmed no-show

Clear statuses prevent incorrect assumptions.

#9. When baggage is delayed, damaged or missing

Baggage problems are common pressure points because they affect both the individual guest and the wider coach movement.

Zayed International Airport advises passengers with delayed, damaged or lost checked baggage to contact the relevant airline’s baggage-services desk on arrival; the airline manages the checked-baggage claim.

Official reference: Zayed International Airport baggage services

The DMC representative should support the guest without pretending to control the airline’s baggage process.

#Practical scenario

Forty-two of 44 delegates are ready.

Two delegates are reporting missing bags.

The main coach has already waited for 20 minutes.

The welcome meal at the hotel is approaching its closing time.

#Primary response

The airport representative should:

  1. Confirm that the two guests are safe.
  2. Help them locate the airline baggage desk.
  3. Confirm that the baggage report is being completed.
  4. Ensure the hotel address and guest contact details are accurate.
  5. Keep one representative with them.
  6. Activate a recovery vehicle.
  7. Release the main coach at the approved time.
  8. Inform the hotel and control room.
  9. Record the baggage reference information when the guest agrees.

#Suggested message for the affected delegates

We will remain with you while the baggage report is completed. The main coach will continue so the other delegates are not delayed. Your separate hotel transfer is arranged, and the hotel team has been informed.

#Suggested message for the main group

Two delegates are receiving assistance with delayed baggage. A member of our team will remain with them, and their transfer has been arranged separately. We will now continue to the hotel.

#Backup plan

The airport operation should identify:

  • Baggage-case representative
  • Recovery vehicle
  • Hotel contact
  • Airline-claim support procedure
  • Maximum coach waiting time
  • Guest essentials plan
  • Follow-up owner
  • Client reporting method

#When the guest has no essential items

A delayed bag may contain:

  • Medication
  • Conference clothing
  • Presentation materials
  • Chargers
  • Personal-care items

The representative should not make promises about baggage delivery.

The team can still help by:

  • Confirming the hotel delivery address
  • Informing the hotel desk
  • Identifying nearby essential-item options
  • Escalating medication concerns appropriately
  • Notifying the conference team about missing presentation materials
  • Recording agreed service-recovery support

The emotional impact matters.

For the operations team, it is one delayed bag.

For the delegate, it may feel like the beginning of a very difficult trip.

#10. When a delegate cannot find the meeting point

Even with good signage, some delegates may leave the arrivals area without finding the event representative.

Possible reasons include:

  • The sign uses the agency name instead of the conference name.
  • The guest exits through a different path.
  • The guest does not recognise the event logo.
  • The delegate follows a colleague.
  • The meeting-point message was not received.
  • The phone has no local connection.
  • The sign is hidden behind another group.
  • The guest goes directly to a taxi or ride-hailing pickup.

#Practical scenario

The manifest shows 38 delegates.

Thirty-seven are at the meeting point.

The missing delegate’s phone rings but is not answered.

A colleague says the delegate collected their bag and left baggage claim.

The coach-release deadline is approaching.

#Primary response

The airport team should:

  1. Confirm the delegate was expected on the flight.
  2. Check whether colleagues saw the guest.
  3. Call and message using the approved contact process.
  4. Check the designated meeting and holding points.
  5. Inform the airport lead.
  6. Check whether the hotel or client has received contact.
  7. Avoid sending the entire team in different directions.
  8. Apply the agreed missing-guest deadline.

#Suggested message to the delegate

Welcome to Abu Dhabi. We are waiting for you at the conference meeting point in the arrivals area. Please reply with your current location or contact our airport representative on this number. Do not leave the airport until we confirm your transfer arrangement.

#Backup plan

At the decision deadline:

  • One representative remains responsible for the missing guest.
  • The main coach is released.
  • A recovery vehicle remains available.
  • The hotel desk is informed.
  • The control room records all contact attempts.
  • The client representative is notified at the agreed escalation level.

#Preventive action

Before travel, send guests:

  • Event name
  • Airport meeting-point description
  • Sign design
  • Representative contact
  • Emergency number
  • Instruction not to leave independently without informing the team
  • Hotel name
  • What to do when the phone has no signal

A photo of the actual event sign can be more helpful than a paragraph describing it.

#11. When a passenger appears but is not on the manifest

Unexpected guests require a welcoming response and a controlled verification process.

The representative should not embarrass the delegate in front of the group.

At the same time, the team should not place an unidentified passenger on a coach without checking:

  • Event entitlement
  • Hotel allocation
  • Security considerations
  • Vehicle capacity
  • Client approval
  • Data accuracy

#Practical scenario

A passenger approaches the conference sign and presents the event invitation.

Their name is not on the airport manifest.

The passenger says:

My registration was confirmed yesterday. The client told me the transfer was included.

The coach has one available seat but limited luggage space.

#Primary response

The representative should say:

Welcome. Let me confirm your registration and hotel arrangement so that we place you on the correct transfer.

The team should then:

  1. Check the latest manifest.
  2. Contact the client’s authorised representative.
  3. Confirm hotel allocation.
  4. Check vehicle and luggage capacity.
  5. Confirm whether the guest has special requirements.
  6. Record the addition.
  7. Update the hotel desk.
  8. Obtain approval when an additional movement is required.

#Backup plan

Possible outcomes include:

Add the guest to the planned coach

Use when registration, hotel and capacity are confirmed.

Use the recovery vehicle

Use when the main coach has insufficient capacity or the hotel allocation differs.

Hold the guest temporarily

Use when client confirmation is still pending and a safe, comfortable waiting arrangement is available.

Arrange an individual transfer

Use only after the appropriate commercial or client approval.

#What not to say

Avoid:

Your name is not here, so you cannot come with us.

Use:

I am checking the latest registration information to make sure we send you to the correct hotel.

The operational control remains firm.

The tone remains welcoming.

#12. When a confirmed delegate does not arrive

A no-show should not be declared too early.

The delegate may have:

  • Missed the flight
  • Rebooked
  • Travelled on another flight
  • Changed arrival date
  • Left independently
  • Failed to complete travel documentation
  • Cancelled without the DMC receiving the update

#Practical scenario

The manifest shows 45 delegates.

Only 44 are accounted for.

The airline arrival status shows the flight has arrived.

The missing person’s colleague says:

I think they missed the connection, but I am not sure.

#Primary response

The representative should not wait indefinitely based on an uncertain statement.

The team should:

  1. Check client and airline updates available to the operation.
  2. Attempt approved contact.
  3. Ask the colleague for any confirmed information.
  4. Check whether the delegate was moved to another flight.
  5. Inform the control room.
  6. Release the main movement at the agreed time.
  7. Keep the delegate listed as unresolved until confirmed.

#Backup plan

The control room should prepare for three possibilities:

The delegate arrives on a later flight

Assign the guest to a later compatible wave or recovery vehicle.

The delegate arrives the next day

Create a new movement and update hotel and registration arrangements.

The delegate does not travel

Cancel or release unnecessary services according to supplier terms.

#Commercial note

The client agreement should explain:

  • No-show charges
  • Flight-change handling
  • Additional transfer costs
  • Late notification
  • Unused vehicle charges
  • Overnight airport staffing

Operational flexibility should not create uncontrolled financial exposure.

#13. When a connection is missed or the flight moves overnight

A missed connection can change an afternoon delay into a next-day operation.

It can affect:

  • Hotel room nights
  • Airport staffing
  • Vehicle schedules
  • Registration
  • Meals
  • Speaker programmes
  • Conference access
  • Welcome materials
  • Guest confidence

#Practical scenario

A conference speaker misses a connection in Europe.

The original Abu Dhabi arrival was 18:00.

The new arrival is 07:30 the following morning.

The speaker is scheduled to participate in a panel at 10:00.

#Primary response

The project manager should create a recovery plan covering:

  • Confirmed replacement flight
  • Airport representative
  • Meet-and-assist requirement
  • Priority baggage support
  • Direct airport-to-venue or airport-to-hotel decision
  • Clothing and preparation needs
  • Speaker materials
  • Vehicle type
  • Venue access
  • Client approval
  • Backup speaker plan

#Possible plan A: Airport to hotel

Use when:

  • The speaker needs to shower and change.
  • The hotel can prepare the room.
  • Travel time allows venue arrival.
  • Presentation materials are available.

#Possible plan B: Airport directly to venue

Use when:

  • Timing is extremely tight.
  • The speaker agrees.
  • Luggage can be handled separately.
  • The venue has changing or preparation facilities.
  • Event organisers are ready to receive the speaker.

#Possible plan C: Remote or replacement participation

Use when:

  • The new arrival cannot meet the programme.
  • The client has a virtual-participation option.
  • Another speaker can cover the session.

#Suggested message to the speaker

We have received your revised flight information and are preparing the fastest practical arrival plan. A representative will meet you at Zayed International Airport. We are confirming whether the best option is to travel first to the hotel or directly to the venue, and we will send the final plan before departure.

#Backup plan

Critical speakers should have:

  • Direct contact details
  • Flight monitoring
  • Priority airport support
  • Dedicated vehicle option
  • Presentation backup
  • Client decision authority
  • Alternative session plan

A speaker’s arrival should not be managed as a normal group transfer when the conference programme depends on that person.

#14. When a guest requires mobility or special assistance

Accessibility planning should begin before arrival day.

Zayed International Airport recommends requesting wheelchair assistance through the airline at booking or at least 48 hours before the flight. The airport states that special assistance is available around the clock, accessible pickup parking is available, and accessible taxis may be arranged or pre-booked. It also provides an on-site medical clinic operating 24/7.

Official reference: Zayed International Airport special assistance

The DMC must connect airport assistance with the onward ground movement.

Airport wheelchair support does not automatically mean that the assigned coach, hotel entrance or event venue is suitable.

#Practical scenario

A delegate arrives using wheelchair assistance.

The airport team knew that assistance was required inside the terminal.

However:

  • The assigned coach is not accessible.
  • The guest cannot climb the coach steps safely.
  • The hotel desk has not been informed.
  • The guest’s companion is assigned to another coach.

#Primary response

The airport lead should:

  1. Keep the guest and companion together.
  2. Confirm the guest’s preferred and safe transfer method.
  3. Activate the accessible vehicle.
  4. Avoid unsafe lifting or improvised assistance.
  5. Inform the hotel.
  6. Confirm the hotel arrival route.
  7. Update the conference accessibility record.
  8. Release the main coach when necessary.

#Suggested guest message

Your accessible transfer is being prepared, and your companion will travel with you. We have informed the hotel team so that they can receive you through the appropriate entrance.

#Backup plan

The pre-arrival checklist should connect:

  • Airline assistance
  • Airport assistance
  • Personal mobility equipment
  • Accessible ground transport
  • Companion travel
  • Hotel-room requirements
  • Hotel entrance
  • Venue route
  • Emergency contact
  • Return transfer

#Practical scenario: a guest feels unwell

A delegate becomes dizzy after reaching the meeting point.

The airport team should not immediately place the guest on a coach.

#Response

  1. Check for immediate danger.
  2. Contact airport medical support according to official procedures.
  3. Keep the guest accompanied.
  4. Inform the airport lead.
  5. Protect privacy.
  6. Delay the guest’s transfer until medically appropriate.
  7. Arrange a companion and recovery vehicle.
  8. Inform the hotel only with suitable consent and necessary information.
  9. Record the incident factually.

The airport’s on-site medical clinic is available 24/7 for emergencies or necessary support.

The operations team supports the guest.

Medical decisions remain with qualified professionals.

#15. When VIPs and speakers arrive with the main group

VIP service should be clear, discreet and operationally realistic.

A VIP movement may involve:

  • Personalised meet-and-assist
  • Fast-track services where booked and available
  • Dedicated vehicle
  • Separate luggage support
  • Direct hotel contact
  • Security liaison
  • Private check-in
  • Speaker schedule coordination

Zayed International Airport offers Salam Meet & Assist services, with options described by the airport including personalised assistance, luggage service and fast-track support. Availability and exact inclusions should be confirmed for the booking.

Official reference: Zayed International Airport Salam service

#Practical scenario

A keynote speaker and two senior client executives arrive on the same flight as 46 delegates.

The original plan assigns everyone to one coach.

After landing, the client asks for the three VIP guests to be taken directly to the hotel in a separate premium vehicle.

No premium vehicle is waiting.

#Primary response

The airport lead should avoid making an unconfirmed promise.

The team should check:

  • Is a suitable vehicle available?
  • What is its arrival time?
  • Is the main group being delayed?
  • Is the hotel ready for private reception?
  • Where is the VIP luggage?
  • Who approves the additional cost?
  • Will the VIP movement affect another service?

#Backup plan

The operation should identify three service levels before arrival:

Standard group movement

VIP guests travel with the delegation but receive priority assistance and seating.

Enhanced group movement

VIP guests use the same vehicle but receive individual meet-and-assist and hotel reception.

Dedicated movement

VIP guests receive a separate vehicle and personalised handover.

The service level should be agreed commercially before arrival.

#Suggested client response

We can arrange a dedicated vehicle, subject to immediate availability and approval of the additional service. The current estimated positioning time is 20 minutes. Alternatively, the guests can depart immediately on the confirmed coach with priority hotel reception.

This gives the client options without creating a false promise.

#16. Control vehicle staging, loading and release

The airport team should not call a coach for loading simply because some guests are ready.

The movement should be released when:

  • The correct delegate group is identified.
  • The hotel split is confirmed.
  • The vehicle is correct.
  • The driver is confirmed.
  • The host is present.
  • Luggage capacity is suitable.
  • Missing guests have a clear status.
  • The loading point is available.
  • The hotel is ready.
  • The movement controller approves release.

#Practical scenario

Forty delegates are ready.

Four are still collecting baggage.

The coach enters the loading position.

The driver says the vehicle cannot remain for long.

The airport representative begins loading the 40 guests without recording which four are missing.

#The risk

After departure, the team may not know:

  • Which guests were left behind
  • Which hotel they belong to
  • Whether they have luggage
  • Who is supporting them
  • Which vehicle will collect them
  • Whether the hotel has been informed

#Primary response

Before release, record:

  • Expected delegates
  • Presented delegates
  • Boarded delegates
  • Missing delegates
  • Names of missing delegates
  • Current status of each missing delegate
  • Recovery owner
  • Recovery vehicle
  • Hotel notification

#Backup plan

A release decision might read:

Wave A04 expected 44 delegates. Forty boarded. Four remain at baggage claim and are in contact with Representative R2. Main coach released at 14:20. Recovery minicoach M3 assigned. Hotel Two informed. Next update due at 14:35.

This is operational control.

“Four people are coming later” is not.

#Public pickup versus group-coach arrangements

The airport’s normal arrivals pickup guidance directs vehicles to the Short Stay Car Park. Large group coaches, branded movements and managed event pickups may require different operational arrangements. These should be confirmed in advance with the relevant partners rather than improvised on arrival day.

#17. Protect luggage during split movements

When a group is split between vehicles, luggage can easily become separated from the correct guest or hotel.

#Practical scenario

A delayed baggage case causes two delegates to remain at the airport.

Their checked bags did arrive and were loaded onto the main coach by colleagues.

The delegates later travel in a recovery car.

At the hotel, nobody knows whether the bags are:

  • On the coach
  • At the airport
  • With another delegate
  • In the hotel luggage room

#Primary response

When delegates and luggage travel separately, record:

  • Guest name
  • Movement code
  • Bag count
  • Bag tag or identifying information
  • Vehicle carrying the luggage
  • Hotel receiving contact
  • Guest carrying essential items
  • Handover confirmation

#Backup plan

Use hotel- or movement-based luggage labels.

For example:

  • BLUE — Hotel One
  • GREEN — Hotel Two
  • GOLD — Hotel Three
  • RED — Hotel Four

The label should not reveal sensitive personal information unnecessarily.

For major events, consider:

  • Dedicated luggage vehicle
  • Luggage manifest
  • Numbered luggage tags
  • Hotel receiving zone
  • Signed handover
  • Separate oversized-equipment list

A guest can forgive a delayed coach more easily than a missing suitcase caused by an uncontrolled transfer.

#18. Keep guest communication warm, simple and honest

Airport representatives often know too much operational detail.

Delegates need only the information that helps them understand what happens next.

A good airport message explains:

  • What is happening
  • What the guest should do
  • Where the guest should wait
  • Who is helping
  • When the next update will come

#Avoid operational language

Do not say:

Dispatch has not released the coach because the supplier has a vehicle rotation issue.

Say:

Your coach has been slightly delayed. Please remain with our team at the meeting point. We are confirming the revised loading time and will update you again at 14:15.

#Practical scenario

The coach is delayed by 25 minutes.

The representative says:

It will be here in five minutes.

Ten minutes later, the coach has still not arrived.

The representative repeats the same promise.

Guests begin to lose confidence.

#Primary response

Give a confirmed update instead of a hopeful estimate:

Your coach is on the way, with the latest confirmed arrival at 14:30. Seating is available nearby, and our team will update you again at 14:20.

Even when there is no new information, keep the promised update time:

The coach is still approaching the airport. The latest confirmed arrival remains 14:30. Thank you for your patience; we will begin preparing the group for loading shortly.

#Backup communication library

Prepare messages for:

  • Early flight
  • Delayed flight
  • Baggage delay
  • Missing delegate
  • Coach delay
  • Replacement vehicle
  • Split movement
  • Unexpected guest
  • Hotel-not-ready situation
  • VIP change
  • Medical assistance
  • Overnight rebooking

The message should be:

  • Short
  • Calm
  • Accurate
  • Respectful
  • Consistent across channels

#19. Escalate airport problems using FACT

Airport situations often become confused because employees send incomplete messages.

Use the FACT structure:

#F — Facts

What has been confirmed?

#A — Affected movement

Which guests, flight, hotel or vehicle are affected?

#C — Clock

When must a decision be made?

#T — Treatment options

What realistic responses are available?

#Practical scenario

A representative sends:

The bags are taking too long. The coach is waiting. Please advise.

The control room does not know:

  • How many guests are ready
  • How many bags are delayed
  • How long the coach has waited
  • Whether another vehicle is available
  • When the coach must leave

#Better escalation

Wave A05 expected 48 delegates. Forty-three guests and all their luggage are ready. Five delegates are still waiting for checked bags. The coach has been at the loading point for 15 minutes and must release by 16:40. Recovery minicoach M2 can collect the remaining five guests at 17:00. We recommend releasing the main coach at 16:40 and keeping Representative R4 with the remaining guests. Approval required by 16:35.

Now the control room can make a decision.

#Backup plan

The escalation chart should identify:

Level 1: Local correction

Example: replacing a damaged event sign.

Level 2: Airport lead coordination

Example: moving staff between simultaneous flight waves.

Level 3: Control-room or client approval

Example: activating an additional paid vehicle.

Level 4: Emergency response

Example: a medical or security incident.

Each level should identify:

  • Decision maker
  • Contact method
  • Financial authority
  • Response time
  • Documentation requirement

#20. Complete a proper airport-to-hotel handover

The airport movement does not end when the coach leaves.

It ends when the receiving hotel confirms:

  • The vehicle arrived
  • The guest count is correct
  • Any missing guests are known
  • Separate luggage is accounted for
  • VIP arrangements are active
  • Accessibility support is ready
  • Open baggage cases are recorded
  • The next programme information has been communicated

#Practical scenario

A coach leaves the airport with 47 delegates.

The control room records the movement as complete.

At the hotel, the desk expects 49 delegates.

Two guests are travelling separately after a baggage delay, but the hotel desk was not informed.

The hotel team begins treating them as missing guests.

#Primary response

The airport representative or onboard host should send a handover message:

Wave A03 departed AUH at 13:40 with 47 delegates for Hotel Two. Two delegates remain with Representative R6 completing baggage reports and will travel on Minicoach M1. Their expected hotel arrival is 14:35. One bag for Guest X is on the main coach and will be held by the hotel desk.

The hotel should acknowledge receipt.

#Backup plan

Use three confirmations:

Departed

Sent by the airport team.

Arriving

Sent when the vehicle is approaching the hotel.

Received

Sent by the hotel desk after physical arrival and count.

This closes the movement.

Without hotel confirmation, the control room knows that a coach departed.

It does not yet know that the guest journey was completed.

#21. Hand over between airport shifts without losing information

A long arrival programme may require more than one airport shift.

A weak shift handover can cause:

  • Repeated guest calls
  • Forgotten baggage cases
  • Uncollected passengers
  • Duplicate vehicles
  • Missing signs
  • Unapproved overtime
  • Incorrect hotel information

#Practical scenario

The morning airport lead finishes at 16:00.

A delayed flight originally expected at 14:30 is now arriving at 18:00.

The handover message says:

The afternoon team will handle the delayed flight.

It does not explain:

  • Which representative is assigned
  • Which coach will operate
  • Whether the hotel knows
  • Which guests are VIPs
  • Whether dinner is affected
  • Whether overtime was approved

#Primary response

The handover should include:

  • Open flight waves
  • Revised arrival times
  • Assigned staff
  • Vehicle allocation
  • Hotel contact
  • VIP and accessibility notes
  • Missing guests
  • Baggage cases
  • Client approvals
  • Additional costs
  • Next decision deadlines
  • Guest messages already sent

#Backup plan

The incoming shift should repeat back the critical information.

For example:

Confirmed: I am taking ownership of Wave A07. Revised arrival is 18:00. Representative R8 and Coach C12 are assigned. Two VIP guests require separate hotel reception. Hotel Four has acknowledged the delay. Dinner boxes are being held. Next flight-status check is at 16:30.

A handover is complete only when the new owner accepts responsibility.

#22. Measure the airport operation properly

An airport operation should not be judged only by whether coaches eventually left.

Useful performance indicators include:

  • Flight waves staffed on time
  • Delegates met successfully
  • Guests who could not find the meeting point
  • Manifest discrepancies
  • Average passenger-release time
  • Coach waiting time
  • Recovery vehicles activated
  • Baggage cases supported
  • Missing-guest resolution time
  • Accessibility requests delivered correctly
  • Guest communication updates delivered on time
  • Hotel handovers completed
  • Additional services approved correctly
  • Supplier response time
  • Complaints relating to airport arrival
  • Incidents escalated before the decision deadline

#Practical scenario

All 500 delegates eventually reach their hotels.

The project report says:

Airport operation completed successfully.

However, a deeper review shows:

  • Twelve guests could not find the sign immediately.
  • Two coaches waited for more than 45 minutes.
  • Three hotel desks received incorrect arrival counts.
  • One mobility request was missed.
  • Five separate guest messages contained different instructions.
  • Additional vehicles were activated without written approval.

The operation was completed.

That does not mean the system performed well.

#Improvement plan

The post-event review should ask:

  • Which flight waves caused the most pressure?
  • Were staffing levels appropriate?
  • Did signs work?
  • Were vehicle decisions made early enough?
  • Did guests receive consistent information?
  • Were hotels ready?
  • Were baggage and accessibility cases handed over properly?
  • Which backup resources were used?
  • Which backup resources were missing?
  • Which costs should be included in the next quotation?

The purpose of measurement is not to blame the airport team.

It is to make the next arrival programme easier to control and better for the guest.

#The idea behind a strong airport arrival operation

A MICE airport operation is not successful because a representative held a sign and a coach eventually departed.

It is successful when:

  • Sales provides usable information.
  • Every flight has an owner.
  • Delegates are divided into controlled waves.
  • Airport staff know their roles.
  • Flight changes are detected early.
  • Hotels receive accurate updates.
  • Missing guests have a clear recovery process.
  • Baggage cases do not delay entire groups unnecessarily.
  • Accessibility is connected to ground transport.
  • VIP service is agreed before arrival.
  • Coaches are released using confirmed counts.
  • Guests receive calm and honest messages.
  • Every airport movement is closed with a hotel handover.

The delegate may never see the control board.

They may never know that a vehicle was replaced, a flight wave was divided or a hotel arrival time was changed.

What they should feel is much simpler:

Someone was waiting.

The sign was easy to find.

The representative knew their name.

The instructions were clear.

The transfer was comfortable.

When something changed, somebody explained what would happen next.

That is the real purpose of MICE arrival-wave control at Zayed International Airport:

To turn an unpredictable international arrival process into a warm, organised and dependable welcome to Abu Dhabi.
