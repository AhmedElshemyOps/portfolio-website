Build a practical control system from process mapping and risk assessment through SOP writing, implementation, audit, CAPA and governance.

    Procedures, Processes and Process Maps: What Is the Difference?

    A process explains how value moves from an input to an intended result. A procedure explains how work is controlled and performed. A process map makes the flow, decisions, and handoffs visible.

  2. 02
    Operational Excellence & SOPs · 12 min read

    Never Write an SOP Before Mapping the Process

    An SOP written from memory usually documents the official story. A process map built from observation reveals the real work, hidden handoffs, workarounds, delays, and decisions the procedure must control.

  3. 03
    Operational Excellence & SOPs · 12 min read

    How to Use SIPOC in Tourism Operations

    A practical way to set process boundaries, expose missing inputs, and agree what a tourism service must deliver before anyone writes the detailed SOP.

  4. 04
    Operational Excellence & SOPs · 11 min read

    How to Conduct an Operational Risk Assessment Before Writing an SOP

    Risk assessment should shape the procedure before the procedure is written. It identifies what could affect the objective, who may be harmed or impacted, which controls already exist, what remains uncertain, and where authority or escalation is required.

  5. 05
    Operational Excellence & SOPs · 12 min read

    How to Write a Professional SOP: Part 1 — Structure

    A professional SOP is not a long checklist and not a policy essay. It is a controlled operating document that connects purpose, scope, roles, procedure, exceptions, records, measures, approval, and revision.

  6. 06
    Operational Excellence & SOPs · 11 min read

    How to Write a Professional SOP: Part 2 — The Procedure

    Turn policy intent and mapped work into instructions that identify the actor, action, sequence, decision, time standard, evidence, and exception path.

  7. 07
    Operational Excellence & SOPs · 11 min read

    SOP Writing Part 3: Roles, Authority and RACI

    Define who performs the work, who owns the outcome, who contributes before a decision, who receives information, and where operational authority stops.

  8. 08
    Operational Excellence & SOPs · 14 min read

    SOP Writing Part 4: Exception and Escalation Management

    A procedure becomes operationally credible when it explains not only the normal path, but also how people recognize a deviation, stabilize the service, obtain authority, communicate, record decisions, recover, and learn.

  9. 09
    Operational Excellence & SOPs · 11 min read

    SOP Writing Part 5: Evidence, Documentation and Legal Defensibility

    Design records that capture timely, factual, proportionate evidence of what happened—without turning operational documentation into improvised legal conclusions.

  10. 10
    Operational Excellence & SOPs · 14 min read

    Document Control & Version Management

    A procedure creates confidence only when people can find the right document, recognize its status, use the current approved version, record the work correctly, and prevent obsolete instructions from returning to the operation.

  11. 11
    Operational Excellence & SOPs · 11 min read

    How to Audit an SOP

    Assess whether an SOP is current, understood, followed, evidenced, effective, and still suitable—without reducing the audit to a document-presence checklist.

  12. 12
    Operational Excellence & SOPs · 11 min read

    CAPA: Corrective and Preventive Action in Tourism

    Turn complaints, incidents, audit findings, defects, and adverse trends into controlled actions that contain the problem, remove verified causes, and prove the change works.

  13. 13
    Operational Excellence & SOPs · 11 min read

    Root Cause Analysis for Tourism Managers

    Move beyond the visible service failure to verify the process, information, capacity, authority, system, supplier, or management condition that allowed it to occur.

  14. 14
    Operational Excellence & SOPs · 11 min read

    Building the Tourism Operations Manual

    Create a navigable governance and operating architecture across reservations, transport, airports, excursions, representatives, suppliers, safety, quality, finance, after-hours work, and crisis response.
